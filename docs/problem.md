# Problem Identification & Relevance
## The Problem

When contaminated food hits grocery stores, workers still rely on slow paper records to find the bad batch. Because they cannot pinpoint which box is bad, stores throw away entire shelves of perfectly good food just to be safe.

## Why It Matters

Food recalls are rising, and shoppers panic:

* **Recalls are spiking:** In August 2026 alone, Salmonella alerts jumped sixfold across 26 FDA recalls.
* **Shoppers stop buying:** 67% of people stop buying a whole food category (like all bagged spinach) after just one recall. [1]
* **Good food gets tossed:** 59% of consumers throw away safe groceries at home because they cannot check their batch code.
* **Widespread fear:** 94% of shoppers actively worry about food safety.

> "Consumers are making a category-level decision about a product-level problem."
> — **GS1 US**

## How RubyChain Solves This

RubyChain uses secure digital tracking from the farm to the store checkout. Instead of clearing out the whole store, workers can fix the problem in seconds:

* **Stop only the bad batch:** One click blocks the dangerous lot at checkout. Safe items stay on the shelf and keep selling.
* **Ditch paper records:** Digital tracking replaces lost paperwork and human error.
* **Protect customer trust:** Shoppers can scan an item on their phone to verify it is safe before buying or throwing it out.
* **Meet government rules:** Automatically meets new FDA safety rules well before the 2028 deadline.
